<p class="answer">Mate Translate is a general-purpose translator for Mac, iPhone and web browsers that translates text you select in any app. Mailkin does one thing: it translates emails automatically inside Apple Mail, on your Mac. If you mainly need email translated, Mailkin is less work; if you translate everywhere, Mate covers more.</p>

## At a glance

<div class="table">

| | Mate Translate | Mailkin |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Translator app and browser extensions | Apple Mail extension |
| How email is translated | Select text, then use the shortcut or menu | Automatically when you open the email |
| Whole email in place, with formatting | No, translated text shown separately | Yes |
| Where translation happens | Online translation services (an offline mode is part of Mate Pro) | On your Mac, Apple's translation |
| Languages | 100+ | 25 |
| Works outside Mail | Yes: Safari, Chrome, any app, iPhone | No, Apple Mail on Mac only |
| Price | Free tier; Mate Pro subscription | 30 emails free, then €14.99 / US$14.99 once |

</div>

Mate details are from Mate's own App Store listing and help pages as of November 2026 and may change; check [matetranslate.com](https://matetranslate.com/) for current features and prices.

## When Mate is the better choice

- You translate in many places: web pages, documents, chats, not just email.
- You need a language outside Apple's 25 on-device languages.
- You also want translation on iPhone or in a browser.

## When Mailkin is the better choice

- Your foreign-language text arrives mostly by email, in Apple Mail.
- You want emails to open already translated, without selecting anything.
- You prefer your email never to leave your Mac.
- You'd rather pay once than subscribe.

The two don't conflict: some people use Mailkin for their inbox and a general translator for everything else.
