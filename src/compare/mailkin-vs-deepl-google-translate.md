## Steps and formatting

With a web or desktop translator, each email is a small routine: select the text, copy it, switch to the translator, paste, then switch back to Mail to reply. DeepL's Mac app shortens this with a shortcut (press ⌘C twice on selected text), but you still select the text each time. Pasted text loses the email's layout, so tables, links and images don't come along.

Mailkin translates the email when you open it. The subject and body appear in your language in the same Mail window, with the layout intact, and a banner shows the source language with Show Original one click away.

## Privacy

DeepL and Google Translate translate online, so the text you paste is sent to their servers.

- **DeepL free:** DeepL's privacy policy says texts submitted to the free translator are kept for a limited period and may be used to train and improve its models.
- **DeepL Pro:** the policy says Pro texts are not permanently stored, are kept only temporarily to provide the translation, and are not used to improve DeepL's services.
- **Google Translate:** the consumer service is covered by Google's general [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

Mailkin uses Apple's on-device Translation framework, so the email content never leaves your Mac. That makes a difference for contracts, medical letters or anything you would rather not paste into a website.

## When DeepL or Google Translate is the better choice

- You need a language outside Apple's 25 on-device languages.
- You translate more than email: web pages, documents, chat messages.
- You only get a foreign-language email now and then, and a quick paste is enough.
- You use a mail app other than Apple Mail, or a Windows PC.

## When Mailkin is the better choice

- Foreign-language emails arrive regularly in Apple Mail.
- You want to read the email as it was sent, with links, tables and images.
- You want some senders always translated, others never, and per-language rules.
- You prefer that email content never leaves your Mac, without an account.
- You'd rather pay once than subscribe.
