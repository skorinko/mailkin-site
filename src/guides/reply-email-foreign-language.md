<p class="answer">To reply to an email in a language you don't speak, write your reply in your own language, then translate it: in Apple Mail on a Mac, select the text, Control-click, choose <strong>Translate</strong> and click <strong>Replace with Translation</strong>. Keep the reply short and plain, and consider keeping your original text below it.</p>

## First, make sure you understand the email

A good reply starts with reading the original properly. In Apple Mail you can select the text, Control-click it and choose **Translate**. If foreign-language email is a regular thing for you, [Mailkin](/translate-email-apple-mail/) shows incoming emails already translated in Mail, on your Mac.

Mailkin translates the emails you receive. It doesn't translate emails you write or reply to, at least not yet, so for the reply itself use one of the methods below.

## Translate your reply in Apple Mail

macOS can replace selected text with its translation, and this works in Mail's compose window:

1. Click **Reply** and write your answer in your own language.
2. Select the text you want to translate.
3. Control-click the selection and choose **Translate "…"**.
4. In the pop-up, check that the target language is the one your correspondent writes in.
5. Click **Replace with Translation**. Your text is swapped for the translation.

If you want offline translation, or translation that stays on your Mac even when you're online, download both languages in **System Settings › General › Language & Region › Translation Languages** and select **On-Device Mode** ([how](/guides/download-translation-languages-mac/)).

## Polish your own text first with Writing Tools

On Macs with Apple Intelligence turned on, **Writing Tools** can proofread your draft and rewrite it in a friendly, professional or concise tone. In Mail you can open Writing Tools from the toolbar or by holding the pointer over selected text. Apple Intelligence isn't available on every Mac or in every language or region.

Use it on the draft in your own language, before translating. Clear, correct input gives a much better translation.

## Other ways

- **Copy into an online translator** such as DeepL or Google Translate. These can be strong, but the text is sent to that service, so avoid it for anything personal or confidential.
- **Ask a colleague or friend** who speaks the language to check anything important.

## Tips for a reply that translates well

Machine translation does best with simple text:

1. **Keep sentences short.** One idea per sentence.
2. **Avoid idioms and jokes.** "Let's touch base" or "no worries" rarely translate cleanly.
3. **Be explicit with dates, times and amounts.** Write "Friday 4 December, 14:00" instead of "this Friday afternoon", and include the currency.
4. **Use names instead of "he", "she" or "it"** when it could be unclear who or what you mean.
5. **Check it by translating back.** Select the translation, choose **Translate**, and read it in your own language. If the meaning changed, simplify the original and try again.
6. **Add your original below.** A line like "Original text in English:" followed by your draft lets the reader check anything that came out oddly.

## Replying in English is often fine

Many people who write to you in their language read English well, especially in business, travel and online shopping. A short, friendly opener helps:

> Thank you for your email. I'm replying in English, as I don't speak German. I'm happy to switch if you prefer.

This is often clearer than an imperfect translation. If the sender then replies in English too, the problem is solved for both of you.
