## Productivity: Mailbutler

[Mailbutler](https://www.mailbutler.io/apple-mail/) is an Apple Mail extension that adds email tracking (opens and link clicks), message templates, signatures, tasks and notes, and on higher plans AI writing help such as smart replies and summaries. It also works in Gmail and Outlook, which suits teams on mixed setups.

**Price:** per-user subscription, Starter US$5/month up to Business US$32/month, cheaper billed yearly; free 14-day trial. **Worth it if** you send a lot of business email and want templates and follow-ups in Mail. **Keep in mind** that read tracking relies on tracking pixels, which some recipients block.

## Spam filtering: SpamSieve

[SpamSieve](https://c-command.com/spamsieve/) from C-Command is a long-running spam filter for the Mac. It learns from the messages you mark as spam or good, keeps allow and block lists, and works with IMAP, Exchange and POP accounts. Filtering happens on your Mac and it doesn't need your mail password. Its site lists support through the current macOS.

**Price:** one-time US$39.99 (family licence US$63.98), with a 30-day trial. **Worth it if** your provider's spam filter lets too much through.

## Filing: MsgFiler 4

[MsgFiler 4](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/msgfiler-4/id6478043112?mt=12) files Apple Mail messages into mailboxes, mostly from the keyboard, so you don't drag messages around the sidebar. It's for people who keep a tidy folder structure and want to get to inbox zero quickly.

**Price:** free download; premium features by subscription (US$0.99/month or US$9.99/year) or a US$49.99 lifetime unlock. Also sold directly by the developer.

## Privacy: MailTrackerBlocker

[MailTrackerBlocker](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mailtrackerblocker/id6450760473) detects and blocks hidden tracking pixels in HTML email, recognising hundreds of email marketing services, and shows a 30-day report of what it blocked.

**Price:** US$6.99 on the Mac App Store, no subscription listed. **Keep in mind:** the developer notes Mail allows one active message security extension, so its tracker report can't run alongside a GPG extension.

## Encryption: GPG Mail

[GPG Mail](https://gpgtools.org/), part of GPG Suite, adds OpenPGP encryption, decryption, signing and verification to Apple Mail. It's the established choice if you exchange encrypted email.

**Price:** from GPG Mail 8 it's sold as a yearly support plan, with a 30-day trial for new users. **Keep in mind:** check the developer's site for the current state of macOS support before installing; it has published notes about a Mail crash on macOS Tahoe that Apple addressed in a later release.

## Translation: Mailkin

[Mailkin](/translate-email-apple-mail/) is our app, so weigh this entry accordingly. It's a Mail extension that translates incoming emails when you open them, in place, keeping layout, links and images, in [25 languages](/languages/). Translation uses Apple's on-device translation, so email content never leaves your Mac, and there's no account. It doesn't translate emails you write, and it's Mac only (macOS 26 or later).

**Price:** 30 translated emails free, then a one-time €14.99 / US$14.99 ([details](/buy/)). **Worth it if** you regularly get email in other languages. If you only need a translation now and then, the free Translate command built into macOS may be enough; see [Mailkin vs macOS Translate](/compare/mailkin-vs-macos-translate/) and [Mailkin vs Mate Translate](/compare/mailkin-vs-mate-translate/).

## How to choose

1. **Start with what bothers you most.** Spam, clutter, tracking or a language barrier: pick the one tool for that job.
2. **Prefer one-time prices for simple jobs.** Subscriptions make sense for services with ongoing server costs, like tracking and AI features.
3. **Check where your email goes.** Some add-ons process mail on your Mac; others use the developer's servers. The developer's privacy policy says which.
4. **Add one extension at a time** so it's clear what changed if Mail behaves differently.
