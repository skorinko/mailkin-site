<p class="answer">Mailkin is for parents whose children go to a school that writes in another language, and for families who keep in touch with relatives abroad. It translates incoming emails automatically in Apple Mail, on your Mac, so you can read the school newsletter or a message from grandparents in your own language.</p>

## The situation

Your children attend a local school in a new country, or an international school where some messages come in the host country's language. Every week there's something: a newsletter with dates for the term, a note from the class teacher about a trip, a reminder to sign a permission form, a message about lunch payments.

At the same time, family email may come in a language you don't read well. Your partner's parents write in Italian, a cousin in Turkish, or an aunt in Russian sends photos and news. You want to understand them properly, not just guess from a pasted translation.

## How Mailkin helps

- **School emails open translated.** Choose the school's language once, and every email in it is shown in your language when you open it.
- **Newsletters keep their pictures and links.** Photos, calendars, tables of dates and links to forms stay in place, with a banner saying which language it was in.
- **Family emails, your way.** Choose **always translate** for a relative whose messages you need help with, or **never translate** for one you'd rather read in the original, for example to practise the language.
- **See the original any time.** **Options › Show Original** shows the exact wording, useful for names, dates and anything you want to quote.
- **Private family email stays private.** Mailkin uses Apple's on-device translation, so your family's messages are never sent to a translation service.

## An example

On Friday afternoon the school sends its weekly newsletter in French. It has a photo of the class garden, a short list of dates for next month, a reminder about a museum trip and a link to a form for parents. With Mailkin, you open it and read it in English, with the photo, the dates and the link where they were. You spot that the trip form is due on Wednesday and fill it in that evening.

## Setting it up for school and family

1. [Download Mailkin](/download) and choose the language you want to read email in.
2. Add the school's language, for example French, German, Spanish, Dutch or Japanese, and set it to **automatic**.
3. Add the languages your relatives write in, such as Italian, Russian, Turkish, Polish or Arabic. Set them to **automatic**, or to **only when you ask** if you understand most of what they write.
4. If your children's school sends some emails in English too, you don't need to do anything: only emails in the languages you've chosen are translated.

## Good to know

- **Replies aren't translated.** Mailkin translates emails you receive. To reply to a teacher, draft your message and use the Translate command in macOS. A short reply in simple words is usually understood well.
- **Official documents need care.** Machine translation is for understanding. For school contracts, medical forms or official records, get a professional translation where accuracy matters.
- **Mac only**, in Apple Mail on macOS 26 or later, with iCloud, Gmail, Outlook or Exchange, and IMAP accounts.
- **Licensing.** 30 translated emails are free, then €14.99 / US$14.99 once per person, for every Mac you use. The App Store version will support Family Sharing once it's live.
