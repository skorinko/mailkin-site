<p class="answer">Mailkin is for small businesses that handle customer email in Apple Mail and hear from customers in several languages. It translates each incoming message automatically on your Mac, so you can understand a question in Spanish, Japanese or Polish as soon as you open it.</p>

## The situation

You sell online, run a small app, or rent out holiday homes, and your customer support is a shared inbox in Apple Mail. Most messages come in English, but a growing share don't. A customer in Madrid asks about a refund in Spanish. Someone in Osaka writes in Japanese because a setting doesn't work. An order question arrives in Polish.

You don't want to lose those customers, and you don't want to paste their names, addresses and order numbers into a website to find out what they're asking.

## How Mailkin helps

- **Customer emails open translated.** Choose the languages you hear from, and each email in those languages is shown in your language when you open it.
- **Order details stay readable.** Forwarded order confirmations, tables and links keep their layout, and a banner shows the original language.
- **Check the customer's words.** **Options › Show Original** shows the exact text, handy for copying an order number, a name or an error message.
- **Translate the rare ones on demand.** Languages you rarely see can be set to **only when you ask**.
- **Customer data stays on your Mac.** Translation uses Apple's on-device translation, nothing is sent to a translation service, and you don't need an account. Your mailbox is never modified.

## An example

A customer writes in Spanish: they were charged twice for an order and include the order number and a screenshot of their bank statement. With Mailkin, you open the email and read it in English straight away, with the screenshot in place. You switch to the original to copy the order number into your shop's admin, then write your reply.

## Setting it up for a support inbox

1. [Download Mailkin](/download) and choose the language your team reads email in.
2. Add the languages your customers use most, for example Spanish, French, German, Portuguese or Japanese, and set them to **automatic**.
3. Set less common languages to **only when you ask**, and translate those emails from **Options** when they arrive.
4. Choose **never translate** for internal senders, payment notifications or tools that already write in your language.

## Good to know

- **Replies aren't translated.** This matters most for support. Mailkin helps you understand the customer; to answer in their language, draft your reply and use the Translate command in macOS, or reply in English if that works for your customers.
- **Each person needs their own license.** The one-time €14.99 / US$14.99 license is per person and covers every Mac that person uses. If three people answer support email, that's three licenses. There's no team or volume pricing.
- **Machine translation is for understanding.** For legal complaints, chargebacks or anything with legal weight, get a professional translation where accuracy matters.
- **Mac and Apple Mail only**, on macOS 26 or later. Mailkin doesn't work in help desk tools or webmail.
