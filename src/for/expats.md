<p class="answer">Mailkin is for people who have moved to another country and now get important email in a language they're still learning. It translates those emails automatically inside Apple Mail, on your Mac, so you can read a letter from your landlord, your child's school or the tax office in your own language as soon as you open it.</p>

## The situation

Moving abroad means a lot of new paperwork, and much of it now arrives by email. The rental agency sends a note about a meter reading. The health insurer confirms a change to your policy. The bank asks you to update your details. The town hall replies to your residence permit question. The school sends a newsletter about an excursion.

Each one is written in the local language, often in formal wording that is harder than everyday conversation. You can copy each email into a translator, but that is slow, it loses the tables and links, and you may not want to paste your bank or insurance details into a website.

## How Mailkin helps

- **Emails open already translated.** Choose the local language once, and every email in that language is shown in your language when you open it in Apple Mail.
- **The layout stays intact.** Tables in a utility bill, links to a portal, logos and images stay where they were. A banner at the top tells you which language the email was in.
- **The original is one click away.** Use **Options › Show Original** to see the exact wording, for example to copy a reference number or quote a sentence back.
- **Your email stays on your Mac.** Translation uses Apple's on-device translation, so letters from your bank, doctor or authorities are never sent to a translation service.
- **It works with every account in Mail.** iCloud, Gmail, Outlook or Exchange, and other IMAP accounts all work the same way.

## An example

Say you live in Germany and your landlord's management company emails about the annual service charge statement. The email has a short paragraph, a table of costs, and a link to download the full statement. With Mailkin, you open it and read the paragraph and the table in English, with the link still working. The banner shows the email was in German. If you need to call them, you switch to the original to read out the reference number exactly as written.

## Setting it up for life abroad

1. [Download Mailkin](/download) and choose the language you want to read email in.
2. Add the local language of your new country, for example German, Spanish, French, Italian or Dutch, and set it to **automatic**. macOS downloads it once from Apple.
3. If you still get personal email in a language you read well, set that language to **never** so it is left alone.
4. Turn on Mailkin in **Mail › Settings › Extensions**. If you are learning the language and want to try reading some senders yourself, choose **never translate** for those senders and keep translation on for the rest.

## Good to know

- **Machine translation is for understanding.** It is very useful for knowing what a letter says and what you need to do. For contracts, court or immigration documents and anything official, get a professional translation where accuracy matters.
- **Replies aren't translated.** Mailkin translates emails you receive, not emails you write. For a reply, draft it and use the Translate command in macOS.
- **Mac only.** Mailkin works in Apple Mail on macOS 26 or later.
- **Very long emails** (over 20,000 characters) are translated when you choose **Options › Translate This Email**.
