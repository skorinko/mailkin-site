<p class="answer">Mailkin is for importers, buyers and sourcing managers who deal with suppliers that write in their own language. It translates incoming quotes, order confirmations and shipping notices automatically in Apple Mail, on your Mac, with tables and links kept in place.</p>

## The situation

Many suppliers write to you in English, but not all of them, and not always. A factory in China sends a revised quote with a note from the sales manager in Chinese. A Japanese manufacturer confirms an order in Japanese. A German machine maker sends a delivery schedule, and an Italian producer forwards a message from their logistics partner about a delayed container.

These emails matter: a changed unit price, a new lead time or a missing document can cost you money. Pasting each one into a translator takes time, breaks the tables, and sends prices and terms to an outside service.

## How Mailkin helps

- **Supplier emails open translated.** Choose the languages your suppliers write in, and each email in those languages is shown in your language as soon as you open it.
- **Quotes and confirmations keep their shape.** Price tables, item lists, tracking links and attached images stay in place, so you can scan a translated quote the way you'd read the original.
- **Check the exact wording.** **Options › Show Original** shows the email as sent, which helps when you need to match a part number or quote a clause back to the supplier.
- **Rules per sender.** Always translate a supplier who sometimes switches languages, or never translate a contact whose English emails you'd rather read untouched.
- **Commercial terms stay private.** Translation runs on your Mac with Apple's translation. Nothing is sent to a translation service, and there's no account.

## An example

A supplier in Shenzhen replies to your request for quotation. The email opens with a few lines in Chinese explaining that raw material costs have gone up, followed by a table of items, quantities and new unit prices, and a link to their catalogue. With Mailkin, you read the explanation and the table in English, and the banner tells you it was in Chinese. Before you approve the order, you switch to the original to compare the item codes against your purchase order.

## Setting it up for sourcing

1. [Download Mailkin](/download) and choose the language you read email in.
2. Add the languages your suppliers use, such as Chinese, Japanese, German, Italian, Korean, Turkish or Vietnamese, and set them to **automatic**.
3. If some partners write in a language you only see now and then, set it to **only when you ask**, so you translate those emails on demand.
4. Set **always translate** for suppliers who mix languages, and **never translate** for your freight forwarder or bank if they write in a language you already read.

## Good to know

- **Machine translation is for understanding.** Use it to follow quotes, schedules and updates. For contracts, certificates, customs paperwork and anything binding, get a professional translation where accuracy matters.
- **Replies aren't translated.** Mailkin translates emails you receive. To write back in the supplier's language, draft your reply and use the Translate command in macOS.
- **One license per person.** The one-time €14.99 / US$14.99 license covers every Mac you personally use. Colleagues need their own; there's no team pricing.
- **Mac only**, in Apple Mail on macOS 26 or later. Signed emails are left untouched, and your mailbox is never modified.
