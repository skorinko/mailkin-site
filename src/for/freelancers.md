<p class="answer">Mailkin is for freelancers and small agencies whose clients don't always write in their language. It translates incoming briefs, feedback and invoices automatically in Apple Mail, on your Mac, so you can understand a client's email the moment you open it.</p>

## The situation

You might be a designer with a client in France, a developer working for a startup in Spain, or a two-person studio with customers in Germany and Brazil. The project lead writes to you in English, but the marketing manager sends feedback in French. The client's accounts department sends a remittance notice in Portuguese. A new lead from Italy writes in Italian because that's easier for them.

You want to answer quickly and get the details right. Copying each email into a translator slows you down, scrambles the formatting of a brief, and means pasting confidential client material into a website.

## How Mailkin helps

- **Client emails open translated.** Pick the languages your clients use, and their emails appear in your language as soon as you open them in Apple Mail.
- **Briefs keep their structure.** Bullet points, tables, links to shared files and embedded images stay in place, with a banner showing the original language.
- **The original is always there.** **Options › Show Original** shows the client's exact words, useful for checking a product name or a phrase they want on the website.
- **Fix the language if needed.** If a short email is detected as the wrong language, you can correct it.
- **Confidential work stays on your Mac.** Mailkin uses Apple's on-device translation; nothing is sent to a translation service and there's no account.

## An example

A client in Lyon sends feedback on a website draft. The email has a numbered list of changes, a link to a shared folder and two screenshots, all in French. With Mailkin, you open it and read the list in English, with the screenshots and the link where they were. One comment asks for a specific tagline, so you switch to the original to copy the French wording exactly.

## Setting it up for client work

1. [Download Mailkin](/download) and choose the language you read email in.
2. Add the languages your clients write in, such as French, Spanish, German, Italian or Portuguese, and set them to **automatic**.
3. If you read one of your clients' languages well enough, set that language to **never**, or choose **never translate** for that client only.
4. For a newsletter or notification you don't need translated, choose **never translate** for that sender to keep it in the original.

## Good to know

- **Replies aren't translated.** Mailkin translates what you receive, not what you write. For replies, the Translate command in macOS can help with a draft. For proposals and website copy, have a native speaker check the final text.
- **Contracts need care.** Machine translation is for understanding. For contracts, terms and anything legally binding, get a professional translation where accuracy matters.
- **One license per person.** €14.99 / US$14.99 once, after 30 free translated emails, covers every Mac you personally use. Each member of an agency needs their own license.
- **Mac only**, in Apple Mail on macOS 26 or later, with iCloud, Gmail, Outlook or Exchange, and IMAP accounts.
